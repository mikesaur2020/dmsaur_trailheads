# Vision

**Every great product begins with a real problem.**

DMSaur Trailheads exists to catch good problems before they disappear — the
everyday frustrations mentioned once and forgotten — and to give the people who
surface them recognition, on their own terms, when their insight helps inspire a
real product.

We don't ask people to design an app. We ask them to describe a problem,
frustration, need, or opportunity. What to build is DMSaur's job to figure out.

> Ideas are easy. Learning is the adventure.

## The long-term idea pipeline

An idea that resonates can travel a full arc. Each stage adds understanding —
the point is learning, not just shipping.

1. **Problem** — Someone describes a real frustration or unmet need, in their
   own words. This is the raw material for everything that follows.
2. **Evidence** — The community responds with meaningful signals: I have this
   problem, I would use a solution, I would help test it, I would pay for it.
   Evidence is richer than a popularity vote.
3. **Validation** — Promising problems are examined more closely. Is the pattern
   real? Who exactly is affected, how often, and what do they do today?
4. **Prototype** — A rough, testable attempt at a solution, shaped with input
   from contributors who volunteered to help.
5. **Build** — A validated prototype becomes a real product effort.
6. **Release** — The product reaches people who have the original problem.
7. **Learning** — Outcomes feed back into the pipeline. What we learn — even from
   the problems we *don't* build — makes the next journey better.

## Principles

- **Problems first.** Never optimize for clever app ideas over real problems.
- **Contributors matter.** Recognition is a choice, privacy is a default, and
  appreciation is never a competition.
- **Evidence guides.** Community signals and real-world context — not hype —
  point to what's worth solving.
- **Honesty about status.** Sharing an idea is never a promise to build it.

## What this is not

- Not an issue tracker or a feature-request leaderboard.
- Not a place to pitch finished product designs.
- Not a guarantee of development for any submission.
